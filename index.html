    How to use

    Every keyboard shortcut. Most only work on the Design tab, when you are not typing in a box and no window is open.

    The quick way

    Click Furniture presets (the dark button at the left of the ribbon), pick a design in the middle, set your size on the right, and press Create. Every size has a smallest and a largest value: a box turns red when a number is outside them, and a line says what it must be. If your numbers cannot work together, a warning box says what is wrong, why, and what to change, and nothing is built until they can. Then open the Cut list & shopping tab for the shopping list, the cut list and the drawings of each part, and the Build instructions tab for the steps that put the parts together. Print either one from File, then Print.

    New to woodworking? explains every name the program uses for a piece (apron, cleat, slat) and shows how each joint is made, with a drawing. On the pages, a highlighted word can be clicked for the same thing.

    Where things are
    Tabs
    The numbered tabs along the top are the steps of a project: Design, Cut list & shopping, Build instructions. File, at the far left, has New, My projects, Save, Copy and Paste design as text, Load and Print.
    Furniture presets
    The window has three parts side by side. On the left, Start here (good first projects: the ones rated "Beginner: a good first project"), Recent (what you made last in this browser), Favorites, All designs and the groups, each with how many designs it holds. In the middle, the designs of the group you picked; type in Find a design to look through all of them by name, by what they are made of or by what they are for, and click the buttons beside it (Beginner-friendly, Outdoor and so on) to narrow the list. Click the star on a design to add it to Favorites. On the right, the design you picked: its live picture, which changes as you type, its sizes and choices, and a box that says what cannot work and why. Left of the buttons it says how hard it is to build and about how long it takes. The skill is a rule of thumb: doors to hang, drawers to fit, many plywood panels, angled cuts, carrying fish tanks and framing a building make a design harder; a pocket-hole jig does not, and neither do wooden plugs over screw heads (they only add time). Doors and drawers you add yourself count too, once their hinges or slides are on. The design on the right can always be found in the middle: when a search or a filter leaves it out, it waits at the top of the list, marked "Chosen, not in this list", until you pick another. The sizes you type for a design are kept while you look at others; Reset sizes, beside its name, puts them back where they started. Keyboard: the window opens with the cursor in Find a design; type, then Enter picks the first design found and moves to its sizes, and Enter there presses Create, also right after you put a red size right. The arrow keys move through the designs, and Esc in the list clears what you typed before it closes the window. The window is laid out for a page of 1024 x 640 or larger; in a smaller window its three parts slide sideways together.
    Ribbon
    The row of commands under the tabs, in groups: Start, Add, Tools, Edit, View and Finish. More at the end of a group opens every command of that group. Display switches between realistic wood and color blocks, makes the wood see-through or shows the fasteners only, opens all doors and drawers, turns on the exploded view and the color key, and turns the move arrows on or off. A command that is gray needs something first, usually a selected piece.
    Panels
    The Pieces list and the Properties of the selected piece float over the 3D view. Drag one by its title bar, roll it up with the dash, or hide it. The two buttons at the right edge of the view bring them back. Properties appears when a piece is selected.
    Status bar
    The bar along the bottom says what to do next with the current tool and shows live sizes while you drag. It also has the overall size, the piece count, a warning when pieces overlap, the Fasteners and Sizes switches, and the Snap size.
    Dialogs
    A window that pops up over the view, such as Furniture presets, Style & finish or Mirror. The dark button on the right does the job, and Enter presses it. Cancel, the X in the corner and Esc all close it without changing anything. Hold the pointer over a box to read a tip about it.
    Beginner and Expert
    The switch at the top right. Beginner shows the three steps a first project needs, and the program picks every screw and joint for you. Expert adds one more tab, Fasteners, after Design: a table of every place two pieces meet, where you can change the screw or joint by hand. Nothing is missing in Beginner: the hardware to buy and which fastener goes where are on the other pages.
    Pages to print
    Every tab after Design is a page to read on screen or print. On those tabs the ribbon holds the commands of the page: Back to design, Print, and Style & finish, which picks the wood, the finish and how fancy the joints are. On the Cut list & shopping tab, type the prices from your store and the total follows.
    Build step by step
    In the Finish group. The 3D view then shows the design as it stands after each step: what is built so far, the pieces of this step in orange, and the fasteners of this step in dark blue, right through the wood. A panel shows the words of the step and what you need for it. Back and Next, or the left and right arrow keys, move through the steps; Esc leaves and puts the view back as it was. The steps always follow the design: change, add or delete a piece and they are worked out again, here and on the Build instructions page. A design you drew yourself gets a suggested order.
    Check my design
    In the Tools group, under More (or right-click empty space in the view). It looks for pieces that nothing holds up, boards too long for their load, and anything that could tip or lean over, and says what to change. Every preset passes it as it comes; run it after you change a design by hand or draw your own. These are rule-of-thumb checks, not an engineer's certification.
    Long fish tank walls
    A fish tank wall (or cabinet stand) longer than the longest board you can haul is still built as one wall: the rails of each row are made of pieces joined end to end over a pair of uprights (one upright when they are 4x4s), with a cross piece just inside each rail end, the two cross pieces screwed to each other, and the plywood deck running across the joint, and there are posts at the two ends only, so the tanks stay evenly spaced. The pieces are as even as the bays allow. Build it as in the preset's form can make it separate sections you can carry instead, each with its own posts; they are stood side by side where the rack goes and screwed together through the posts that meet, with no glue, so they come apart again. Either way every upright stands on one piece of deck, so it gets one set of pocket screws. These are rule-of-thumb checks, not an engineer's certification.
    Prices
    The prices in the shopping list start as estimates: national averages, not a quote from any store. Update prices asks the national lumber price index how much boards and plywood have moved and moves the estimates; apart from counting visits on the public site (see Counting visits, below), it is the only time the program goes online, and it sends nothing about your design. Stores do not let a program read their prices, so the exact ones are typed by hand: on the Cut list & shopping tab, look an item up at your store and type its price into the box. A price you type is marked "yours", is remembered, and is never changed by an update. Price book lists every price, and Paste prices reads them from lines copied from a receipt.
    Another device
    A design stays in the browser it was made in. To move it, click File, then Copy design as text, and send the text to yourself in an email or a note. On the other device click File, then Paste design from text. This online copy cannot print or download files, so move a design to the copy on your computer this way to print it.
    Building your own
    Draw a board
    Pick a material in the Add group of the ribbon, click Draw a board, and drag on the floor. Hold Shift to stand it upright.
    Move
    Click a piece, then drag it. It snaps to other pieces. Shift + drag raises or lowers it. Alt turns snapping off.
    Arrows and rings
    A selected piece shows red, green and blue arrows: drag one to slide it in just that direction. They are see-through until the pointer is on one, so they do not hide the piece, and a selected door, lid or drawer shows its open handle instead. You never need them: a piece can also be dragged, moved with M (then X, Y or Z), or placed by typing X, Y, Z in Properties. Display, then Move arrows, turns them off. Click Rotate to get rings instead, and drag a ring to turn the piece to any angle. The colors match the X, Y, Z symbol in the corner of the view.
    Box select
    Hold Shift and drag across empty space to select everything inside the box.
    Copy and mirror
    In the Edit group of the ribbon: Duplicate, Copy in a row (evenly spaced copies) and Mirror (the matching other side). More has Duplicate and drag.
    Shape a piece
    Select one piece and press Change shape under Shape in Properties. Each end can be cut Angled (set the angle the miter saw reads, and which edge keeps the full length), Rounded or Pointed. Add hole and Add cut-out put an opening in the piece: type its size, and how far its center is from an end and an edge, the way you would mark it with a tape. Corners (on plywood, or in Expert) can be cut off, rounded or notched; Expert also adds slots and notches in an edge. A value that cannot be cut turns its box red and says why. The length in the cut list stays the blank you cut first; the cut list page then has a drawing of each shaped piece under Shapes to cut, with how to cut it. Screws and other fasteners keep 3/8" away from holes and cuts; when a joint has no room left for them, Check my design says so. An end that is angled, rounded or pointed only touches the piece it meets: the program works out no screws there, so fasten it by hand. Make it square again takes every cut off (Ctrl+Z puts it back).
    Right-click
    Right-click a piece for a menu of everything you can do to it. Right-click empty space for view options.
    Hide and see through
    H hides the selected pieces, Shift+H shows only them, Alt+H brings everything back. Display, then Exploded view, pulls the pieces apart. Show me, beside a word under "Words used in this design" on the pages, shows where those pieces are: they turn blue and the rest goes see-through until you click.
    Screws and other fasteners
    Every screw, pocket hole, dowel, brad and bracket is drawn where it goes, at its real size. In Display the Wood row picks how to see them: Solid shows the heads on the surface, See-through (Shift+S) lets you watch a screw pass through one piece into the next, and Fasteners only (Shift+F) leaves just outlines of the wood. Point at a fastener to read what it is and what it goes through. Click one to change the fastener of that connection in the Properties panel, or to delete the connection.
    The style decides what shows
    Style & finish picks how a design is fastened, and you see the change at once in the 3D view, the lists and the steps. In Everyday and Fine furniture nothing shows on the top or the front of furniture for the house: a table, desk or bench top is held on from underneath, with pocket screws driven up through holes on the inside of the aprons and cross rails, and it only rests on the legs. The build step says how: lay the top face down on a blanket, set the frame upside down on it, mark the overhang all round and drive the screws. The sides show no screw heads either: the shelves, rails and bottom of a bookshelf or a cabinet are held to the sides by pocket screws from holes underneath or inside. Where a screw has to go in through a face that is seen (a bed rail into its leg), its head is sunk in a short wide hole and covered: a wooden plug glued in and cut flush, or wood filler under paint. The build step says how, the finishing step says how to cut the plugs flush, and the shopping list buys them. The notes of a design say where any head shows. In Rustic / rough the screws go in from outside, with the heads showing, on the top and the sides too. A workbench, garage shelves and outdoor pieces keep their screws through the top in Everyday, because that is how those are built. A fastener you pick by hand stays as you picked it in every style, and "Screws, heads plugged" can be picked for any joint. A design saved under an older rule is fastened the current way when you open it (a fastener you picked by hand is kept), and a line at the bottom of the window says what changed.
    Hardware
    Hardware, in the Add group, has hinges, drawer slides, pulls, catches, casters and more. Pick one, then click the piece it goes on; a blue preview shows where it will sit. Click a piece of hardware to change its size, drag it to slide it along its piece, press Delete to remove it. It is listed under its piece in the Pieces panel and counted in the shopping list.
    Doors, lids and drawers
    Select a door or a lid and choose Hang this door or lid (Hardware pulldown, right-click menu, or Properties): it gets hinges on the edge next to its frame, a pull and a catch. For a new drawer, choose Drawer under More in the Add group, pick metal slides or wooden runners, and click inside the space it goes in (between two sides, with something above and below): it is built to fit, on real slides, with a pull. For a drawer box you built yourself, select it and choose Mount this drawer on slides; the box needs a 1/2" gap on each side for metal slides. A hinged door moves with its cabinet. To open a door, lid or drawer, double-click it (double-click again to close it). Or select it and drag its blue handle, a ball for a hinge and a cone for a drawer (a click on the handle opens or closes it all the way), or use the Open slider in Properties. Display has Open all doors and drawers (Alt+O). If something is in the way, both pieces turn red and the status bar says what it hits. Opening never changes the cut list.
    Fastened pieces
    Pieces that are screwed, nailed or glued together move and turn together: drag one and the rest come along, shown with a lighter outline. Stretching still changes only the piece you stretch. To move one piece alone, select it and press Unfasten (U): it lists the connections and asks before deleting them. Connect (C) fastens two pieces again. Pieces that end up touching are fastened by themselves; in Expert, the Auto-fasten switch in the status bar turns that off.
    Components
    A component is a named set of pieces, like a door or a whole workbench, and it can hold smaller components. Every preset arrives as one. Select pieces and press Shift+M (Make component) to make your own; Shift+B (Break apart) takes one apart. The Pieces panel lists them as a tree: click a row there to select the whole component, or click a selected piece again in the 3D view to step up to the component around it. In Expert, Pick in the status bar can be set to Component, and then the first click selects the whole component.
    Overlaps
    Pieces that occupy the same space turn red, and a warning appears in the status bar. Click the warning to select them.
    Change length
    Click a piece and drag one of the blue handles on its ends, or type a size in the Properties panel.
    Connect
    Click Connect, then the piece to move, then the piece it should meet. The fastener is suggested for you.
    Tape measure
    Click Tape measure (T), then two points. The pointer catches corners, edges, the middles of edges, faces and the floor. The size stays on screen, with how far across, up and front to back, and it stays after you leave the tool until the design changes. Measure an angle (A) takes three points: the end of one line, the corner, the end of the other line. Gap between two pieces (G) takes two pieces and gives the smallest gap between them. Esc cancels a half-made one; Delete clears them.
    Measure
    Ctrl + click two pieces. The Properties panel shows the gap between them.
    Import a cut list
    Found a free plan online? Import a cut list, in the Add group, takes its pieces one per line and lays them on the floor beside your design.
    Look around
    Drag empty space to turn the view, right-drag to slide it, scroll to zoom. Fit view, Front, Side and Top are in the View group.
    Reading a close-up
    Two pictures
    Under each step of the Build instructions, every numbered line has a close-up made of two pictures. How it goes in, on the left, shows the two pieces of one joint at a slant, with the screw or nail in dark blue going through the first piece into the second. Where to mark, on the right, shows the face you mark, drill or nail on, seen straight on, with every size of the line. Click a picture to see it large; click again or press Esc to close it.
    How it is seen
    The line under the title says how you look at the piece: "seen from the back", "seen from the left (from inside)", "underside, seen from below". Left and right in the picture and in the words are as you see the piece that way. Seen from the back, the left end is the one on your left as you stand behind it.
    The heavy line
    The edge or end every size is measured from is drawn heavy and named beside it, such as top end of Back panel (H), or with a slanted line and a dot that point to it. Put your tape on that edge. When the words say "from the nearest edge", the name says so too (the edge nearest the screws): at the other places of the line it may be the other edge. A heavy dashed line is an edge of the piece behind, which you cannot see from where you mark: set that piece in place, or mark where it will sit, first.
    A size
    Each size runs between two thin lines that come off what it measures: an edge of the piece, or the middle of a fastener. The middle of a fastener is a small circle with a cross. The number sits on its own size line; when it does not fit, it is written just past the end of its line. Sizes in one row follow each other: the first from the heavy edge to the first fastener, then from fastener to fastener. 3 spaces at 4 5/8" means three equal gaps of 4 5/8" between four fasteners. Pocket holes whose places the words give one by one (a row kept clear of a rail or of the holes at the ends of an apron, or the holes for a top board that crosses only part of a rail) are each measured from the one heavy end instead: the sizes are stacked, one row for each hole, and every one starts at that end.
    Dashed and broken
    A dashed outline is the piece behind, which you cannot see from where you mark (the rail the nails go into). A dash-dot line is its middle: a row of nails on it lands in the middle of that piece. A zig-zag across a piece means it goes on past the picture; the line under the picture then gives the count of the row. Two zig-zags with white between them mean a length is left out in the middle, so the picture can show the ends large: a size across them still gives the whole distance.
    Pencil marks
    A step that asks you to mark shelf heights shows the marks on its picture, and their heights in a flat picture of its own, "The pencil marks". Each height is measured up from the bottom end, which has a small circle; the number is at the tick of its own mark. The X is on the side of the mark the piece sits on.
    Typing sizes
    How a box shows a size
    Every box you type a size or a position into shows feet and inches once it is over 12": 2'-8", 2'-8 1/2", 25'-0". A size of 12" or less stays in inches: 11 1/2". The cut list, the shopping list, the build instructions and the size labels in the 3D view stay in inches.
    What you can type
    Inches or feet and inches, whichever is quicker: 32, 32", 2'-8", 2' 8, 2'8, 2 ft 8 in, 2.5', 300", 25', with fractions like 3 1/2 or 2'-8 1/2". A plain number is inches. When you leave the box it is written the box's way, so 300" becomes 25'-0" and 32 becomes 2'-8". A minus sign in front of a position or a distance goes the other way. After M or S you can type a size the same way, with the ' and " keys, such as 2'6".
